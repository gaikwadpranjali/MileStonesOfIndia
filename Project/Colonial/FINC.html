1858-1885 CE

The Prelude

The Rise of Political Consciousness

Emergence of the Middle Class
  • British English education created a new class of Western-educated Indian professionals (lawyers, teachers, journalists)
  • This class was exposed to Western ideals of liberty, democracy, and nationalism (e.g., the French and American revolutions)
  • They began to realize the stark contrast between British democratic ideals at home and their authoritarian, exploitative rule in India
Early Political Associations
  • British Indian Association (Calcutta, 1851)
  • Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870), founded by M.G. Ranade
  • Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884), founded by M. Veeraraghavachariar
  • Bombay Presidency Association (1885), founded by Pherozeshah Mehta and K.T. Telang
  • These regional bodies laid the groundwork for a unified, all-India political organization
The Catalyst: The controversial Ilbert Bill (1883) attempt, which sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders, triggered fierce racist backlash from the British community. This exposed the deep racial prejudice of the Raj and convinced Indian leaders of the urgent need for a unified national body.
Dec 28-31, 1885

The Founding Session

A Historic Gathering in Bombay

The First Session
  • Held from December 28 to 31, 1885, at the Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College in Bombay
  • Attended by 72 delegates from across India, representing diverse regions, religions, and professions
  • Womesh Chunder (W.C.) Bonnerjee, a prominent Calcutta lawyer, was elected as the first President
  • The session was meticulously organized by Allan Octavian (A.O.) Hume, a retired British civil servant
Key Resolutions
  • Demanded a thorough inquiry into the working of the Indian administration
  • Called for the expansion of legislative councils and greater Indian representation
  • Advocated for the holding of the Indian Civil Service (ICS) examinations simultaneously in India and England
  • Emphasized the need for national unity, transcending caste, creed, and regional barriers
A Symbolic Beginning: The very act of 72 men from different corners of India gathering under one roof to peacefully debate the nation's future was a revolutionary statement in itself.
1885-1905 CE

Early Objectives

Constitutional Agitation and Economic Critique

Political & Administrative Reforms
  • Expansion and reform of Legislative Councils (Central and Provincial)
  • Separation of the executive and judicial functions to ensure fair trials
  • Reduction of military expenditure, which drained the Indian treasury
  • Repeal of the Arms Act (1878), which disarmed the Indian populace
Economic Critique
  • Reduction of land revenue assessments to relieve the burden on peasants
  • Protection for nascent Indian industries against cheap British imports
  • Abolition of the salt tax, which disproportionately affected the poor
  • Popularization of the "Drain of Wealth" theory, articulating how Britain was systematically impoverishing India
Methodology: The early Congress relied on the "3 Ps" — Prayer, Petition, and Protest. They believed in the British sense of justice and sought reforms through constitutional, lawful, and peaceful means.
Historical Debate

The "Safety Valve" Debate

Was the INC a British Conspiracy?

The "Safety Valve" Theory
  • Promoted by early extremist leaders like Lala Lajpat Rai and later Marxist historians (e.g., R.P. Dutt)
  • Argues that A.O. Hume founded the INC to channel the growing discontent of educated Indians into a harmless, constitutional outlet
  • By providing a platform for "venting," the British hoped to prevent a violent, 1857-style uprising
  • Hume's alleged reference to a "safety valve" in a letter to Calcutta University graduates is often cited as evidence
The "Lightning Conductor" Theory
  • Championed by modern historians like Bipan Chandra
  • Argues that while Hume may have intended it as a safety valve, Indian leaders (like Surendranath Banerjee and Dadabhai Naoroji) actively used it as a "lightning conductor"
  • They harnessed the platform to unite the country, articulate genuine national demands, and build a foundation for the freedom struggle
  • Hume's genuine sympathy for India and his belief in self-governance suggest his motives were more complex than mere imperial manipulation
Historical Consensus: Most modern historians agree that while the British may have initially tolerated the INC as a harmless debating society, the Indian leaders quickly transformed it into a potent instrument of national awakening.
1885-1905 CE

The Moderate Phase

The Pioneers of Indian Nationalism

Key Leaders
  • Dadabhai Naoroji: The "Grand Old Man of India," who articulated the Drain Theory and became the first Asian to serve in the British Parliament (1892)
  • Gopal Krishna Gokhale: A political guru to Mahatma Gandhi, known for his moderate, reformist approach and founding of the Servants of India Society
  • Surendranath Banerjee: A brilliant orator who founded the Indian National Association and later merged it with the INC
  • Firoz Shah Mehta: Known as the "Lion of Bombay," a master of constitutional law and parliamentary procedure
Contributions of the Moderates
  • Successfully exposed the exploitative economic nature of British colonialism
  • Created a sense of all-India national identity, breaking down provincial and communal barriers
  • Trained a generation of Indians in the art of political organization, public speaking, and parliamentary debate
  • Laid the ideological and organizational groundwork for the more radical mass movements that would follow after 1905
A Crucial Foundation: Though often criticized by later extremists for being too mild, the Moderates' greatest achievement was creating a unified, all-India political platform where none had existed before.
LEGACY

Enduring Impact on Indian History

National Unity

Forged the first genuine all-India political identity, transcending regional and religious divides

Political Awakening

Democratized political discourse, bringing the critique of colonialism to the masses through the press

Economic Nationalism

Pioneered the critique of colonial economics, laying the foundation for India's future economic policies

Path to Freedom

Evolved from a moderate debating society into the primary vehicle for India's struggle for Purna Swaraj
