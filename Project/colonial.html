Colonial India

The era of British rule — from the East India Company to the British Raj

The Era of the British Raj

Following the decline of the Mughal Empire, European trading companies vied for control over the subcontinent's vast resources. The British East India Company eventually emerged victorious, transitioning from merchants to rulers.

This era brought profound changes: the introduction of railways, the telegraph, English education, and a unified legal system. However, it also resulted in severe economic drain, the destruction of local industries, and the exploitation of India's wealth, which ultimately sowed the seeds of a massive nationalist uprising.

Key Milestones

1600 CE
Formation of the East India Company

Queen Elizabeth I grants a Royal Charter to the British East India Company, giving it a monopoly on trade in the East Indies and marking the beginning of British commercial interests in India.

1757 CE
The Battle of Plassey

Robert Clive leads British forces to victory against the Nawab of Bengal, Siraj-ud-Daulah. This pivotal battle establishes the British East India Company as a major political and military power in India.

1857 CE
The First War of Independence

Also known as the Sepoy Mutiny, a widespread but ultimately unsuccessful armed uprising against the rule of the British East India Company shakes the foundations of colonial power.

Explore →
1858 CE
The British Raj Begins

Following the 1857 rebellion, the British Crown dissolves the East India Company and assumes direct control over India, marking the official beginning of the British Raj under Queen Victoria.

Explore →
1885 CE
Formation of the INC

The Indian National Congress is founded in Bombay. Initially a moderate platform for educated Indians to seek reforms, it later becomes the vanguard of the Indian independence movement.

Explore →
1905 CE
Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi

Lord Curzon partitions Bengal, sparking massive protests. This triggers the Swadeshi Movement, encouraging the boycott of British goods and the promotion of Indian-made products.
