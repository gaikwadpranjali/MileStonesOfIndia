1946-1949 CE

The Constituent Assembly

A Microcosm of India's Diversity

Formation & Composition
  • First convened on December 9, 1946, under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946
  • Initially comprised 389 members, later reduced to 299 after the Partition of India
  • Elected indirectly by the provincial assemblies, representing diverse regions, religions, castes, and political ideologies
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha served as the interim president, followed by Dr. Rajendra Prasad as the elected President of the Assembly
The Objective Resolution
  • Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on December 13, 1946
  • Laid down the foundational philosophy of the Constitution: sovereignty, democracy, justice, equality, and freedom
  • This resolution was later adapted to become the Preamble of the Indian Constitution
  • Debates were exhaustive, with over 7,638 amendments proposed and discussed
A Democratic Miracle: In a country with massive illiteracy, deep social divisions, and recent traumatic partition, the Constituent Assembly managed to forge a consensus through patient debate and mutual respect.
1947-1949 CE

The Drafting Committee

Architects of the Republic

Leadership & Process
  • The Drafting Committee was formed on August 29, 1947, with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as its Chairman
  • Other key members included N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, and T.T. Krishnamachari
  • The committee studied the constitutions of over 60 countries, borrowing the best features while adapting them to Indian realities
  • The process took exactly 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days, holding 11 sessions over 165 days
Global Synthesis
  • UK: Parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship
  • USA: Fundamental Rights, judicial review, impeachment of the President
  • Ireland: Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)
  • Canada: Federal structure with a strong center
  • South Africa: Procedure for amendment of the Constitution
Ambedkar's Vision: Dr. Ambedkar famously warned the Assembly that while the Constitution could provide a framework for good government, its ultimate success depended on the character of the people and their leaders executing it.
January 26, 1950

Republic Day

The Dawn of a New Era

The Historic Day
  • January 26 was chosen deliberately to honor the Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) declaration of 1930
  • The Constitution officially came into effect, replacing the Government of India Act (1935) as the governing document of India
  • India officially transitioned from a British Dominion to a Sovereign Democratic Republic
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad was sworn in as the first President of India in the Durbar Hall of the Rashtrapati Bhavan
Symbolic Transitions
  • The office of the Governor-General (held by C. Rajagopalachari) was abolished
  • The Indian national flag was formally adopted as the flag of the Republic
  • Citizenship was formally defined, granting equal rights to all residents regardless of religion, caste, or gender
  • Massive celebrations erupted across the nation, marking the final, legal culmination of the freedom struggle
A Peaceful Revolution: Unlike many post-colonial nations that descended into authoritarianism, India's peaceful, legal transition to a republic set a powerful precedent for the developing world.
Core Principles

Fundamental Rights & DPSP

The Moral and Legal Compass of the Nation

Fundamental Rights (Part III)
  • Right to Equality (including the abolition of untouchability and titles)
  • Right to Freedom (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession)
  • Right against Exploitation (prohibition of forced labor and child labor)
  • Right to Freedom of Religion and Cultural/Educational Rights for minorities
  • Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32, called by Ambedkar the "heart and soul" of the Constitution)
Directive Principles (Part IV)
  • Non-justiciable guidelines for the state to establish a welfare society
  • Promotion of equal justice, free legal aid, and the separation of the judiciary from the executive
  • Provision for living wages, humane working conditions, and the protection of the environment
  • Uniform Civil Code and the promotion of international peace and security
Universal Adult Franchise: In a bold, unprecedented move, the Constitution granted voting rights to every citizen over the age of 21 (later lowered to 18), regardless of literacy, property, or social status, defying Western skepticism.
A Masterpiece

The Physical Document

Art, Craft, and Preservation

Creation & Artistry
  • The entire Constitution was handwritten in both Hindi and English by Prem Behari Narain Raizada in a flowing italic style
  • He did not charge any fee for his work, only requesting that his name be inscribed on the last page
  • Each page was beautifully decorated and illuminated by artists from Shantiniketan, including Nandalal Bose, Beohar Rammanohar Sinha, and others
  • The borders and illustrations depict scenes from India's rich historical and cultural heritage, from the Indus Valley to the freedom struggle
Preservation
  • The original copies are printed on specially treated parchment paper to ensure longevity
  • They are preserved in a special helium-filled case in the Parliament Library to prevent degradation from oxygen and moisture
  • The calligraphy and artwork make it not just a legal document, but one of the most beautiful constitutional manuscripts in the world
A Labor of Love: The meticulous craftsmanship of the original document reflects the deep reverence the framers had for the foundational law of the land.
LEGACY

Enduring Impact on World History

Longest Written Constitution

Originally 395 Articles and 8 Schedules, it remains the most detailed national constitution in the world

A Living Document

Amended over 100 times to adapt to changing social, economic, and political realities without losing its core identity

Democratic Resilience

Has successfully guided the nation through wars, emergencies, and massive social transformations while preserving democracy

Global Beacon

Serves as an inspiring model for post-colonial nations striving to balance diversity, development, and democratic rights
