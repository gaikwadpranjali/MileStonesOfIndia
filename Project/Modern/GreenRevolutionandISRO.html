Deep Dive: Explore the transformative 1960s and 1970s — a period when India simultaneously conquered the threat of famine through the Green Revolution and reached for the stars with the foundational years of ISRO.
Mid-1960s

The Green Revolution Begins

From "Ship-to-Mouth" to Self-Sufficiency

The Crisis
  • In the mid-1960s, India faced severe droughts and was heavily dependent on food imports (PL-480 wheat from the USA)
  • The situation was famously described as a "ship-to-mouth" existence, threatening national sovereignty
  • Traditional farming methods could not keep pace with the rapidly growing population
The Agricultural Transformation
  • Introduction of High-Yielding Varieties (HYV) of wheat (e.g., Kalyan Sona, Sonalika) and rice
  • Concurrent expansion of irrigation infrastructure, chemical fertilizers, and pesticide use
  • Initial success was concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh
  • By the early 1970s, India achieved self-sufficiency in food grain production, eliminating the need for massive imports
A National Triumph: The Green Revolution not only saved millions from starvation but also restored national confidence, proving that India could solve its own existential challenges through science and policy.
1960s-1970s

Architects of Change

The Visionaries Behind the Harvest

Key Figures
  • Dr. M.S. Swaminathan: The "Father of the Indian Green Revolution," who adapted Mexican dwarf wheat varieties to Indian conditions
  • Norman Borlaug: The American agronomist and Nobel Laureate whose global HYV seed research was crucial to the movement
  • C. Subramaniam: The visionary Union Agriculture Minister who championed the policy changes and secured cabinet approval for the new agricultural strategy
Institutional Support
  • Establishment of the Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI) as the nodal agency for seed development
  • Creation of the Agricultural Prices Commission (now CACP) to ensure Minimum Support Prices (MSP) for farmers
  • Expansion of rural credit through the nationalization of major banks in 1969, ensuring farmers had access to capital
Global Recognition: In 1987, Dr. M.S. Swaminathan was awarded the first World Food Prize, recognizing his pivotal role in saving over a billion people from hunger.
August 15, 1969

The Birth of ISRO

Reaching for the Stars with a Purpose

Vikram Sarabhai's Vision
  • Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, the "Father of the Indian Space Program," believed space technology must serve national development, not just prestige
  • In 1962, he established INCOSPAR (Indian National Committee for Space Research) under the Department of Atomic Energy
  • The Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) was established in 1963 near the magnetic equator in Kerala
  • On August 15, 1969, INCOSPAR was formally restructured and renamed as the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)
Strategic Objectives
  • Developing satellite technology for communication, television broadcasting, and meteorology
  • Using remote sensing for natural resource management, agriculture, and disaster warning
  • Building indigenous launch vehicle capabilities to ensure self-reliance and reduce dependence on foreign powers
  • Sarabhai famously stated: "There are some who question the relevance of space activities in a developing nation. To us, there is no ambiguity of purpose."
A Humble Beginning: Early rocket components were famously transported to Thumba on bicycles and bullock carts, symbolizing the frugal, determined spirit of India's early space program.
April 19, 1975

Aryabhata: India's First Satellite

A Historic Leap into the Space Age

The Launch
  • Named after the ancient Indian mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata
  • Launched on April 19, 1975, from Kapustin Yar, USSR, using a Soviet Kosmos-3M launch vehicle
  • Built entirely by Indian scientists and engineers at ISRO, weighing 360 kg
  • Its primary mission was to conduct experiments in X-ray astronomy, aeronomics, and solar physics
Subsequent 1970s Milestones
  • Bhaskara-I (1979): India's first experimental Earth observation satellite
  • APPLE (1981): India's first experimental communication satellite (developed in the late 70s)
  • Development of the SLV-3 (Satellite Launch Vehicle), which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite in 1980, making India the 6th space-faring nation
Symbol of Sovereignty: Aryabhata proved that a newly independent, developing nation could master complex aerospace engineering, inspiring generations of Indian scientists.
1970s Onwards

Fostering Scientific Temper

Building Institutions for the Future

Educational & Institutional Growth
  • Establishment of the Indian Institute of Management (IIM) Ahmedabad (1961) and Bangalore (1973)
  • Founding of the Space Commission in 1972, with Satish Dhawan succeeding Vikram Sarabhai as ISRO Chairman
  • Massive expansion of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) laboratories
  • Emphasis on "scientific temper" was enshrined as a Fundamental Duty in the Constitution via the 42nd Amendment (1976)
Self-Reliance (Atmanirbharta)
  • The dual success of the Green Revolution and early space missions fostered a deep national belief in self-reliance
  • Reduced dependence on foreign aid and technology, particularly in critical sectors like food and communications
  • Laid the psychological and infrastructural groundwork for India's economic liberalization and tech boom in the 1990s
A Paradigm Shift: India transitioned from being a recipient of foreign technology and food aid to becoming a creator and exporter of scientific solutions.
LEGACY

Enduring Impact on Modern India

Food Security

Transformed India from a food-deficit nation into a leading global exporter of agricultural products

Space Superpower

Paved the way for modern triumphs like Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan, and the Gaganyaan mission

Human Capital

Created a massive ecosystem of engineers, agronomists, and scientists driving global innovation

Frugal Innovation

Established the "Jugaad" ethos of achieving world-class results with limited resources, a hallmark of ISRO
