Freedom Struggle of India

Discover the inspiring journey of courage, sacrifice, unity and determination that led India to independence.

A Journey Towards Freedom

India's freedom struggle was one of the most significant movements in modern history. For generations, Indians challenged colonial rule through resistance, reform, protest and sacrifice.

Leaders and ordinary citizens from different regions and communities joined together with the dream of building a free and independent India.

A struggle built on courage, unity and sacrifice.

Why India Fought for Freedom

Several political, economic and social factors contributed to India's struggle against colonial rule.

Political Control

Indians sought self-rule and freedom from colonial political control.

Economic Exploitation

Colonial policies created hardship and affected Indian industries and agriculture.

Social Unity

People from different backgrounds united around the cause of independence.

Nationalism

A growing sense of Indian identity inspired people to demand self-government.

1857

The Revolt of 1857

One of the earliest major uprisings against British East India Company rule.

The First Major Uprising

The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against the rule of the British East India Company. Soldiers, rulers and civilians in different regions participated in the resistance.

Figures such as Rani Lakshmibai, Bahadur Shah Zafar, Nana Sahib and Tatya Tope became associated with the uprising.

1857

Revolt of 1857

One of the earliest major uprisings against British East India Company rule.

Major Features
  • Soldiers and civilians participated in the uprising.
  • Major centres included Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow and Jhansi.
  • Bahadur Shah Zafar became an important symbolic leader.
  • Rani Lakshmibai became a prominent leader from Jhansi.
Important Leaders
  • Rani Lakshmibai
  • Bahadur Shah Zafar
  • Nana Sahib
  • Tatya Tope
  • Begum Hazrat Mahal
Significance: The Revolt of 1857 became an important turning point in India's struggle against colonial rule.
1885

Indian National Congress

The formation of an important political platform for India's national movement.

Formation & Role
  • The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.
  • Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee was its first president.
  • It provided a platform for political discussion and national demands.
  • It later became a major force in India's independence movement.
1915

Gandhi's Leadership

Mahatma Gandhi introduced mass movements based on non-violence and civil resistance.

Mahatma Gandhi
  • Returned to India in 1915.
  • Promoted Satyagraha and non-violent resistance.
  • Connected national politics with mass participation.
Principles
  • Truth
  • Non-violence
  • Self-reliance
  • Peaceful resistance
1919

Jallianwala Bagh

A tragic event that deeply influenced India's freedom movement.

Historical Impact
  • The event occurred on 13 April 1919 in Amritsar.
  • British troops fired on a gathering of civilians.
  • The incident caused widespread shock and anger.
  • It strengthened opposition to colonial rule.
1920

Non-Cooperation Movement

A nationwide campaign against colonial institutions.

Main Features
  • Encouraged Indians to withdraw cooperation from colonial institutions.
  • Boycott of government schools, colleges and courts was encouraged.
  • Khadi and indigenous products were promoted.
  • The movement encouraged large-scale public participation.
1930

Dandi March

Gandhi's historic protest against colonial salt laws.

Salt March
  • Gandhi began the march from Sabarmati Ashram.
  • The march ended at Dandi in Gujarat.
  • It became a major symbol of civil disobedience.
  • The movement attracted national and international attention.
1930

Civil Disobedience Movement

A major campaign challenging colonial laws through peaceful resistance.

Objectives
  • Challenge unjust colonial laws.
  • Promote self-rule.
  • Encourage peaceful resistance.
Participation
  • Students
  • Farmers
  • Women
  • Workers
1942

Quit India Movement

A powerful nationwide demand for the end of British rule.

August Movement
  • The movement was launched in August 1942.
  • The Indian National Congress demanded an immediate end to British rule.
  • Gandhi gave the famous call "Do or Die".
  • The movement led to widespread protests across India.

Independence
  • India became independent on 15 August 1947.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru became the first Prime Minister of independent India.
  • India entered a new chapter of its history.
